Signs of Transmission Problems

  • Slipping gears — engine revs but vehicle doesn't accelerate
  • Rough, jerky, or delayed gear shifts
  • Shuddering or vibration when shifting
  • Transmission warning light on the dashboard
  • Burning smell, especially after extended driving
  • Fluid leak (transmission fluid is red or dark brown)
  • Grinding or clunking sounds when shifting
  • Vehicle won't go into gear or stay in gear

The Importance of Transmission Fluid

Transmission fluid lubricates moving parts, acts as a coolant, and transmits hydraulic pressure for shifting. Old, contaminated fluid is the leading cause of transmission failure. Most manufacturers recommend flushing and replacing fluid every 30,000–60,000 miles. In hilly terrain like the Pocono Mountains — with frequent gear changes on steep grades — that interval may be shorter.

Transmission Repair FAQ

Flush or rebuild — how do I know which I need?

A flush is routine maintenance for a healthy transmission. A rebuild is needed when internal components have worn or failed. We diagnose first and give you an honest assessment before recommending major work.

My car slips out of gear — is that the transmission?

Gear slipping is one of the most common signs of a failing transmission. It could be low fluid, a worn clutch, or internal failure. Don't drive far on a slipping transmission — call Mike's at (570) 226-6040.

How long does transmission repair take?

A fluid flush takes 1–2 hours. A full rebuild or replacement can take several days depending on parts availability. We'll give you a clear timeline upfront.

Is it worth repairing an old transmission?

It depends on the vehicle's overall condition and the cost versus value. We'll give you a straight answer — we're not going to push an expensive rebuild on a car that isn't worth it.

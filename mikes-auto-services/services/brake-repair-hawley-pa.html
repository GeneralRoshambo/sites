Warning Signs You Need Brake Service

  • Squealing or squeaking when you apply the brakes
  • Grinding metal-on-metal noise — pads worn through
  • Vibration or pulsating when braking
  • Vehicle pulling to one side when you brake
  • Brake pedal feels soft, spongy, or goes to the floor
  • ABS warning light illuminated on your dashboard
  • Longer stopping distances than usual

How Often Should Brakes Be Serviced?

Brake pads typically last 30,000–70,000 miles depending on driving habits and vehicle weight. In hilly areas like the Pocono Mountains and Pike County, brakes can wear faster due to frequent braking on slopes. We recommend inspecting brakes every 12,000 miles or at every other oil change.

Brake Repair FAQ

How long does a brake job take?

A standard front or rear brake pad and rotor replacement typically takes 1–2 hours. More complex work like caliper replacement may take longer. We'll give you an accurate time estimate before we begin.

Do I need to replace rotors when I replace pads?

Not always. If your rotors are above minimum thickness with no warping or deep scoring, they may just need resurfacing. We'll measure and inspect them and give you an honest recommendation.

What causes brake noise?

Squealing usually indicates worn pads — most have a wear indicator that squeals when it's time to replace. Grinding means the pads are worn through and metal is contacting the rotor — this needs immediate attention.

Is it safe to drive on squeaking brakes?

A light squeal may allow you to drive carefully to our Hawley shop. Grinding or a spongy pedal are more serious — call us immediately at (570) 226-6040.
